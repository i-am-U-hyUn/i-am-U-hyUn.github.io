## Introduction

Along with management strategy, **Process Innovation (PI)** is one of the most popular areas in consulting.
To understand PI, we first need to ask: "What is a process?" and "What is innovation?"

---

## 1. What is a Process?

### The SIPOC Concept

From a Six Sigma perspective, the **SIPOC** framework represents a process as follows:

| Letter | English | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| S | Supplier | Provider of inputs |
| I | Input | Resources fed into the process |
| P | Process | The transformation steps |
| O | Output | Results produced |
| C | Customer | Recipient of outputs |

> SIPOC is also used as a new business development framework and is highly useful for defining processes.
{: .prompt-info }

Regardless of industry, the common principles for a process are:
- **Clear start and end**
- **Independent**
- **Divided into meaningful output units**

### Function-Process Model (FP Model)

While management consulting uses Value Chain and supply chain analysis, practitioners more commonly use the **FP Model**.

The FP Model hierarchy:

```
Function
 └── Mega Process / Process
      └── Sub Process
           └── Elementary Process / Activity
```

| Level | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Function** | A cluster of related tasks; forms the basis of the Value Chain but is not executable | Production Management, R&D, Sales |
| **Process** | An executable unit of work that spans multiple departments | Master Data Management, Organization Management, Quality Inspection |
| **Sub Process** | A component of a Process | — |
| **Elementary Process/Activity** | The smallest meaningful business unit; completed by one person in one place | — |

### Process Definition Document

After structuring the FP Model, a **Process Definition Document** is written. It consists of three parts:

1. **Process Map** — Overview of all processes with interconnections
2. **Process Flowchart** — Sub-process relationships, manual vs. automated tasks, approval branches, trigger points
3. **Detailed Process Description** — Instructions, principles, I/O relationships, responsible parties, KPIs

> Building an FP Model and process definition documents requires significant time and effort. If the goal is simple communication, other tools may be more efficient.
{: .prompt-warning }

## 2. What is Innovation?

### Definitions of Innovation

| Source | Definition |
|---|---|
| Wikipedia | "the process of making improvements by introducing something new" |
| American Heritage Dictionary | "the act of introducing something new or something newly introduced" |
| Merriam Webster | "the introduction of something new; new idea, method, or device" |
| **Peter Drucker** | "change that creates new dimension of performance" |

Common keywords: **change**, **introduction**, **new**

> The essence of innovation = **"Introduction of something new, or the change it brings"**
{: .prompt-tip }

### Why Do Companies Innovate?

Schumpeter (Joseph Schumpeter, 1883–1950) described the drivers of innovation:
- Introduction of new **goods**
- Application of new **production methods**
- Opening of new **markets**

Ultimately, innovation is directly tied to **creating economic value** — the core purpose of any business.
Today, innovation has risen beyond value creation to the level of **innovation for survival**.

> "It is not the strongest that survive, but those that survive are the strongest."

## 3. Management Innovation Methods

Since the 1990s, management innovation techniques have diversified. Key methods:

| Method | Origin | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **TPS** (Toyota Production System) | Japan | Waste elimination, Lean production |
| **Six Sigma** | USA (Motorola → GE) | Quality-focused, statistical approach, DMAIC |
| **TOC** (Theory of Constraints) | — | Bottleneck elimination |
| **BPR** (Business Process Reengineering) | — | Radical process redesign |
| **PI** (Process Innovation) | — | IT-driven process redesign |

## 4. PI Methodology

### What is PI?

> **Redesigning business processes using Information Technology (IT)** to operate more efficiently, profitably, and deliver better service to customers.

### Process Innovation vs. Process Improvement

| Category | Process Innovation (PI) | Process Improvement |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Enterprise-wide, radical redesign | Partial, incremental |
| Direction | Top-Down | Bottom-Up |
| Duration | Long-term | Short-term |
| Degree of change | Radical | Gradual |

PI delivers the greatest results in companies in **growth or maturity stages** of their industry.

### PI Approach: Package vs. In-house Development

| Category | Package (SAP, Oracle) | Process-centric (In-house) |
|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | Top-Down | Top-Down + Bottom-Up |
| **Advantages** | Global standards, faster implementation | Fully customized for the business |
| **Disadvantages** | High license costs | Diverges from global standards, complex maintenance |

> Recently, **cloud-based SaaS ERP** is gaining traction as an alternative.
{: .prompt-info }

---

## 5. PI vs. Six Sigma

| Category | PI | Six Sigma |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Establish a foundation for structural improvement | Continuous improvement of specific process quality |
| **Approach** | One-time, enterprise-wide | Iterative, process-specific |
| **Duration** | Medium to long-term | Short-term |
| **Method** | IT-based redesign | Statistical quality management |

The two are often used **in combination**:
- Medium/long-term tasks → **PI**
- Short-term tasks → **Six Sigma**

## Closing Thoughts

While the PI and Six Sigma boom has cooled somewhat, the **Fourth Industrial Revolution** and the spread of **IoT** suggest PI will gain attention again. Robots and AI are completely transforming traditional manufacturing and operational processes.

Management innovation will continue to be a consulting theme as long as businesses exist.
