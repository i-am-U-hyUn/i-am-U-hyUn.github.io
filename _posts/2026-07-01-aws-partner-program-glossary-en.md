In the cloud industry you'll often hear things like **"we're an AWS BP,"** or **"we just listed an offering on Marketplace."** But if someone asks "what exactly is a BP?" or "how is that different from a regular business offering?", it's surprisingly hard to answer cleanly. Here's a rundown of the AWS Partner Network (APN) terminology every cloud professional should have a handle on.

---

## 1. What Is the AWS Partner Network (APN)?

The **AWS Partner Network (APN)** is the global partner ecosystem AWS runs for consulting firms, ISVs (software vendors), resellers, and training companies to build, market, and sell customer solutions using AWS programs, benefits, and resources.

The two things that matter most: which **Path** a partner enters based on its business model, and which **Tier** it holds, which signals how validated and experienced it is.

---

## 2. Partner Paths — Entry Points by Business Model

AWS organizes partners into 5 **Paths** based on their business model.

| Path | Who it's for | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Services Path** | Consulting, implementation, managed services (MSP), and resale firms | Leads to tier recognition (Select/Advanced/Premier). **Most Korean cloud MSPs fall here** |
| **Software Path** | ISVs building software that runs on or integrates with AWS | Leads to AWS Foundational Technical Review (FTR), Marketplace listing, and ISV Accelerate |
| **Hardware Path** | Device makers integrating with AWS IoT/edge services | Validated via the AWS Device Qualification Program |
| **Training Path** | Companies selling or delivering AWS training | Certified as an AWS Training Partner |
| **Distribution Path** | Invitation-only distributors that recruit/support resell partners | Certified as an AWS Authorized Distributor |

> What people commonly call an **"AWS Partner company"** is, in most cases, a **Services Path** partner.

---

## 3. Partner Tiers — Select / Advanced / Premier

Services Path partners are ranked into 3 tiers based on the number of certified staff, track record (launched opportunities/revenue), and Competencies held. Higher tiers unlock more marketing support, funding, and co-sell opportunities.

| Item | Select | Advanced | Premier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Annual fee** | $2,500 | $2,500 | $2,500 |
| **Accredited individuals** | 4 (2 tech, 2 business) | 8 (4 tech, 4 business) | 20 (10 tech, 10 business) |
| **AWS-certified individuals** | 2 | 6 (3+ at Professional/Specialty) | 25 (10+ at Professional/Specialty) |
| **Launched opportunities** | 3, min. $1,500 total MRR | 20, min. $10,000 total MRR | 50, min. $50,000 total MRR |
| **Extra requirements** | — | Partner Business Plan required | 3+ Competencies (incl. MSP or DevOps/CloudOps), Executive Business Review |
| **Key benefits** | Partner Central access, training, PoC funding | Select benefits + co-branded case studies, APN Blog features, Immersion Days | Advanced benefits + early sponsorship access, recognized as a market "leader" |

---

## 4. Specializations vs. Competencies — an Umbrella Relationship

These two are easy to mix up, but the relationship is actually **inclusion, not overlap**.

- **Specialization**: The **umbrella term** covering every kind of AWS-validated partner expertise — including Competency, Service Delivery, Service Ready, and MSP.
- **Competency**: The heaviest-weight Specialization, validated for a **specific industry (financial services, healthcare, retail) or workload (migration, DevOps, data, security)** through customer success stories and technical review.

In short: **every Competency is a Specialization, but not every Specialization is a Competency.**

---

## 5. The ACE Program and Co-sell

**ACE (APN Customer Engagements)** is the **co-sell pipeline management program** where partners build and manage sales opportunities together with the AWS sales team.

- **Pipeline Manager**: The tool where a partner formally registers and tracks a customer opportunity in AWS Partner Central — the starting point of co-sell.
- **Co-sell**: Advancing a partner's sales opportunity together, leveraging AWS's sales resources and network. As of 2026, ACE activity is a mandatory requirement for renewing Specializations.

---

## 6. ISV Accelerate

**ISV Accelerate** is a **co-sell-only program** for companies (ISVs) building software that runs on or integrates with AWS. It connects them with the AWS sales team to surface new opportunities and shorten sales cycles.

---

## 7. MSP Program (Managed Service Provider)

The **MSP Program** validates a partner's ability to continuously operate and manage a customer's AWS infrastructure. It weighs heavily on 24/7 monitoring, optimization, security, and cost management — and carries enough weight to be a required Competency for Premier Tier.

---

## 8. MDF (Marketing/Market Development Funds)

**MDF** is funding AWS provides — as cash or AWS credits — to help offset a partner's marketing costs (seminars, campaigns, content, etc.). Think of it as AWS **co-investing** with the partner to help build new pipeline.

---

## 9. The AWS Marketplace "Offer" — the Real Meaning of "Offering"

The everyday phrase "offering" (a packaged product/service) has a precise technical counterpart on AWS Marketplace: an **"Offer"** with specific contract and pricing terms attached.

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Public Offer** | A standard, publicly listed offer with fixed pricing/terms — anyone can buy under the same conditions |
| **Private Offer** | A non-public offer where the seller (ISV) presents **custom pricing/contract terms** to a specific buyer |
| **CPPO (Channel Partner Private Offer)** | An offer a **channel partner** — authorized by the ISV with a selling authorization — extends to the end customer at a markup over the ISV's wholesale price. The partner becomes the direct contracting/billing party |

So while "offering" colloquially means "the service package we sell," in the **AWS Marketplace context it specifically means which of Public / Private / CPPO you list and sell it as.**

---

## 10. So What Does "BP" Actually Mean?

AWS doesn't officially use the abbreviation "BP." It's **Korean industry shorthand for "Business Partner,"** and in practice it refers to a company enrolled in the **AWS Partner Network** described above — typically a Services Path partner at the Select, Advanced, or Premier tier.

- "We're an AWS BP" → "We're a company registered in the AWS Partner Network"
- "What BP tier are you?" → "Which tier are you at — Select, Advanced, or Premier?"

That's the practical translation to keep in mind.
